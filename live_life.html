Coffee

I am a humble home barista, not a professional. These notes reflect my own trial-and-error learning. I prefer pour-over and hand-grinding — the ritual is half the pleasure.

☕
Ethiopian Yirgacheffe
Yirgacheffe, Gedeo Zone, Ethiopia
Floral and tea-like. Notes of jasmine, bergamot, and lemon zest. Light body with a silky mouthfeel. The birthplace of coffee produces beans that taste like a garden in bloom.
Light roast. Grind medium-fine for V60. Water at 92 C. 1:15 ratio. Pour slowly in concentric circles.
☕
Colombian Supremo
Huila / Narino, Colombia
Rich and balanced. Notes of dark chocolate, caramel, and red apple. Medium body with a clean, sweet finish.
Medium roast. Grind medium for Chemex. Water at 93 C. 1:16 ratio. Let it bloom 30 seconds.
☕
Panama Geisha
Boquete, Chiriqui, Panama
Ethereal. Notes of peach, mango, jasmine, and honey. Tea-like body with an extraordinarily long finish. The most celebrated coffee variety in the world.
Very light roast. Grind fine for V60. Water at 90 C. 1:14 ratio. Treat it with reverence.
☕
Kenya AA
Central Highlands, Kenya
Bold and vibrant. Notes of blackcurrant, grapefruit, and brown sugar. Full body with a wine-like acidity that lingers.
Medium-light roast. Grind medium-fine. Water at 93 C. 1:15 ratio. Stop the pour before the drip slows.
☕
Sumatra Mandheling
North Sumatra, Indonesia
Earthy and full-bodied. Notes of dark cocoa, cedar, tobacco, and brown spice. Very low acidity, almost syrupy body.
Dark roast. Grind coarse for French press. Water at 94 C. 1:13 ratio. Steep 4-5 minutes.
☕
Guatemala Antigua
Antigua Valley, Guatemala
Smooth and spicy. Notes of dark chocolate, cinnamon, and roasted almond. Medium-full body with a smoky, sweet finish.
Medium-dark roast. Grind medium for Chemex. Water at 93 C. 1:15 ratio. Pairs with a quiet morning.
☕
Jamaican Blue Mountain
Blue Mountains, Jamaica
Perfectly balanced. Notes of sweet herbs, cream, and mild chocolate. Silky body with almost no bitterness.
Medium roast. Grind medium. Water at 92 C. 1:16 ratio. This coffee's virtue is its gentleness.
☕
Costa Rica Tarrazu
Tarrazu, San Jose, Costa Rica
Bright and clean. Notes of tangerine, honey, and toasted pecan. Medium body with a crisp, refreshing acidity.
Medium-light roast. Grind medium-fine for V60. Water at 92 C. 1:15 ratio. Best enjoyed black.

Ethiopian Sidamo
Sidamo Zone, Southern Ethiopia
Rich and complex. Notes of blueberry, strawberry, and dark chocolate. Medium body with a wine-like finish. Natural-processed Sidamo is one of the fruitiest coffees in the world.
Light-medium roast. Grind medium-fine. Water at 91-92 C. 1:14 ratio. Buy from a trusted roaster.

Brazilian Santos
Sao Paulo / Minas Gerais, Brazil
Nutty and sweet. Notes of peanut, milk chocolate, and caramel. Low acidity, medium body, very smooth. The quintessential everyday coffee.
Medium roast. Grind medium. Water at 93 C. 1:16 ratio. Excellent as a base for espresso blends.

Yunnan Coffee
Pu'er / Baoshan, Yunnan, China
Mild and aromatic. Notes of brown sugar, roasted walnut, and subtle citrus. Medium body with a clean, sweet finish. China's own specialty coffee is improving rapidly.
Medium roast. Grind medium for V60. Water at 92 C. 1:15 ratio. Try them before the world discovers them.

Elegant and sweet. Notes of red grape, plum, and black tea. Silky body with a sparkling acidity. Every cup carries a story of resilience.
Medium-light roast. Grind medium-fine. Water at 92 C. 1:15 ratio. A great introduction to African coffees.
Hand-Grinding Tips for Beginners
Grinder: A hand grinder with conical steel burrs (e.g., 1Zpresso, Timemore) is far superior to any blade grinder. Consistency of grind is the single most important factor.
Grind size: Finer = more extraction (more bitterness), coarser = less extraction (more sourness). Adjust by half a turn at a time.
Water: Use filtered water, not distilled. TDS around 150 ppm is ideal. Temperature matters: lighter roasts need lower temps (90-92 C), darker roasts need higher (93-95 C).
Ratio: Start with 1:15 (1g coffee per 15g water) and adjust to taste. Weigh everything.
Bloom: Always pour twice the coffee weight in water first and wait 30 seconds.
Freshness: Beans are best 7-21 days after roasting. Grind immediately before brewing.

LeBron James — My Hero
LeBron James
King James
Lakers #6

I am, without apology, a LeBron James fan. Not because he is the greatest basketball player of all time — though I believe he is — but because of what his story represents.

LeBron was born to a single sixteen-year-old mother in Akron, Ohio. He grew up moving from apartment to apartment, sometimes missing months of school at a time. The odds of a kid from that background becoming anything — let alone one of the most dominant athletes in history — were vanishingly small. And yet he did it, not through luck alone, but through a discipline and work ethic that has never wavered over more than two decades in the NBA.

What moves me most is not the championships or the records, but the consistency. Year after year, season after season, LeBron has shown up. He has taken care of his body with a scientific rigor that borders on obsession. He has adapted his game as he aged, from the explosive athleticism of his youth to the cerebral playmaking of his later years. He has spoken out on social issues when it would have been easier to stay silent. He built a school in his hometown.

And then there is the humor. For all his intensity, LeBron has never lost his sense of playfulness on the court — the exaggerated celebrations, the impromptu dance moves, the way he grins after a ridiculous pass. I have learned from him that even in the most high-pressure moments, it is okay to laugh, to enjoy yourself, to not take everything so seriously. I try to bring that spirit into my own work: when the data won't cooperate or the code keeps breaking, sometimes the best thing to do is step back and smile. LeBron shows that greatness and joy are not opposites — they are companions.

As a student who often feels overwhelmed by how much there is to learn, I find LeBron's example deeply encouraging. He shows that greatness is not a single moment of brilliance but a lifetime of showing up, doing the work, and refusing to settle. I will never be the LeBron James of economics — but I can try to show up every day and give my best effort. That is the lesson I take from him.

Music

Music is the soundtrack of my life. I listen while I code, while I read, while I grind coffee, and while I stare at regression outputs that refuse to be significant.

Chinese-Speaking Artists
♫
Song Zuying
Song Zuying
The voice of Chinese folk music. Her renditions of "La Mei Zi" and "Hao Ri Zi" capture the exuberance and warmth of Hunan culture. Her technique is flawless, but it is the joy in her singing that moves me most.
♫
Jacky Cheung
Zhang Xueyou
The "God of Songs" of Cantopop — and my father's favorite singer. "Wen Bie" and "Yi Qian Ge Shang Xin De Li You" are songs I grew up with. Listening to Jacky Cheung always reminds me of home and of my father singing along in the car.
♫
Andy Lau
Liu Dehua
A legend of Cantopop and one of the hardest-working entertainers in any industry. "Wang Qing Shui" and "Bing Yu" are classics. His dedication to his craft over four decades is itself a kind of art.
♫
Faye Wong
Wang Fei
The "Heavenly Queen" of Mandopop. Her voice has an ethereal quality — "Hong Dou", "Chuan Qi", and "Cong Cong Na Nian" are songs that seem to exist outside of time. She is the closest thing Chinese pop music has to a mystic.
♫
Valen Hsu
Xu Ruyun
The queen of "Yun-style singing" — a whispery, intimate vocal style that feels like a secret being shared. "Ru Guo Yun Zhi Dao" and "Du Jiao Xi" are devastating in their delicacy. She taught me that sometimes the quietest voice carries the most weight.
♫
Han Hong
Han Hong
A voice that seems to come from the Tibetan plateau itself — vast, pure, and unflinchingly powerful. "Tian Lu" and "Tian Liang Le" make me feel the scale of the world. Her philanthropic work is as extraordinary as her music.
♫
Sun Nan
Sun Nan
One of the most powerful male voices in Chinese pop. "Zheng Jiu" and "Bu Jian Bu San" showcase a vocal range and emotional intensity that few can match. His voice fills a room the way a pipe organ fills a cathedral.
♫
Wang Leehom
Wang Lihong
A musical polymath who fused Chinese folk with R&B, hip-hop, and classical. "Long De Chuan Ren" and "Da Cheng Xiao Ai" are anthems of a generation. His chameleon-like ability to reinvent himself across genres is something I admire deeply.
