How to Achieve Technological Leapfrogging: Evidence from China's Self-Strengthening Movement
with Fengming Liu (corresponding author) and Shun Wang
Quantitative History
Presented at the 11th Quantitative History Annual Conference, Yantai, China
While extensive research diagnoses causes of technological backwardness, little examines how nations effectively bridge such gaps. How can late-industrializing economies accelerate technological catch-up? This study reinterprets China's Self-Strengthening Movement (SSM) to reveal how state-directed institutional adaptation compressed development timelines. Historical evidence shows that SSM-era military investments catalyzed catch-up through two mechanisms: strategic human capital accumulation and pervasive cognitive shifts dismantling technological conservatism. These institutional interventions created foundational capacity, enabling China's subsequent rapid electrification despite late entry. The findings illuminate the critical role of deliberate state plasticity in overcoming initial disadvantages. As China now faces its "Deepseek moment" — confronting new technological frontiers — this analysis offers a blueprint for developing economies seeking to engineer accelerated technological convergence. State-facilitated institutional adaptation emerges as a vital driver for latecomer advancement.

The Persson Paradox: Emotion-Driven Accountability Destabilizes Climate Cooperation
with Fengming Liu, Fengsheng Xu and Yanxin Zhang
Political Economy
Can voter anger sustain climate pledges over time? We develop a dynamic theory in which pledges bind successors through domestic emotional accountability. Voters experience anger when policy falls short of inherited pledges, and incumbents strategically prime this anger. A paradox emerges: moderate accountability disciplines, yet strong accountability backfires. When emotional stakes are high, Green incumbents retreat from strategic overpledging to avoid an emotional ratchet that persistently raises voter expectations and poisons the political environment for all future governments, including themselves. Emotional accountability thus collapses the commitment it was meant to secure. The findings imply that weaponized voter emotion can destabilize the very climate cooperation it is supposed to enforce.

Carrier Status, Cousin Offers, and Observability in Marriage-Market Search
with Xuanyu Cai and Fengming Liu
Genetic Economics
How does a positive carrier-screening result change the choice between a confirmed first-cousin offer and unrelated marriage-market search? We depict this choice as a model in which a healthy adult who knows her genotype can surrender a kin offer for a costly search attempt. We derive conditions under which private carrier status raises the reservation cost of leaving the kin option, candidate observability reverses that ordering, and early and late disclosure differ. When candidates infer type from the absence of a signal, disclosure combines rejection with improved beliefs, so its effect on carrier search is ambiguous. We show that the ordering and the monotone disclosure response survive repeated search without recall.

Optimal Block Time with Staking Commitment
with Xuanyu Cai and Shun Wang
Tokenomics
How do protocol rules create commitment in a market where tokens pay no cash flows? We study a Stackelberg game where a staker locks tokens for integer blocks and commits to a deterministic liquidation schedule and a trader reacts to diffusion and jump news under block-dependent execution costs. The trader's unique affine best response makes the staker's schedule the metric projection of a fuel-only benchmark onto a cumulative-sales obstacle, yielding constraint losses and a test of whether the lock-up binds. At a fixed reward rate, every payoff-maximizing block time is strictly interior above a computable threshold. Higher rewards weakly lengthen commitment, while greater belief volatility weakly shortens the optimal block time. Protocol speed and credible commitment are one design problem.
05Under Review
Order Flow as a Resource: MEV, Mining, and the Market for Transaction Sequencing
with Beiyi Luo and Shun Wang
Selected for the 23rd China Financial Research Conference (中国金融年会)
Tokenomics
Does the right to sequence transactions strengthen or undermine blockchain security? We model miners' ordering choices in a Proof-of-Work blockchain. Block value must be weighed against reorganization risk. The equilibrium displays strategic complementarity. Higher aggregate MEV attracts hash power, lowers reorg risk, and reinforces extraction incentives. This yields multiple equilibria: miners either extract no MEV or extract fully. Within a block, bot MEV crowds out retail transactions. In the long run, miner entry is neutral for the within-block allocation. Retail fees do not depend on aggregate hash power. Higher bot MEV therefore raises retail fees at all horizons. The sequencing rights market creates a trade-off between funding security and protecting users.

Transparency, Species Visibility, and the Allocation of Biodiversity Conservation
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
Biodiversity Economics
Why do conservation budgets favor visible species? We study a conservation official allocating a fixed budget between a charismatic and an obscure species. The public observes a transparent report and updates its belief about the official's stewardship quality. The official values public recognition. Her posterior reputation enters utility directly. A unique equilibrium emerges. Transparency shifts protection toward the charismatic species. It also unlocks future funding. Biodiversity first rises and then falls with transparency. A minimum standard for the obscure species restores balance. So does a salience-neutral audit. Our results inform the design of conservation funding policy.

Does Taxonomic Diversity Shape Conservation Incentives under Invasion Risk?
with Mingda Wang and Shun Wang
Biodiversity Economics
How should a planner allocate protection across ecosystems differing in taxonomic dispersion? We depict dispersion through two channels, a portfolio effect compressing shock variance and a niche effect lowering the invasion hazard. The planner keeps biodiversity above a minimum standard, so priority follows a targeting score that peaks at intermediate dispersion when invasion is elastic and at the most diverse otherwise. Liability, avoided-loss payments, and subsidies implement the optimum, and a misallocated quota yields an exact welfare loss.

Political Purges and Elite Networks in Late Han China
with Fengsheng Xu and Yanxin Zhang
Quantitative History
The Later Han state was too weak to observe its own provinces, so its purges could reach only elites the court already knew. Can a ruler purge effectively when he cannot see beyond his capital? We propose a model in which state information about elites is detailed but selective. The court observes social coordination more precisely than military mobilization. Purges target the socially cohesive, spare the armed, and miss latent mobilizers. To test this, we hand code the social ties, military capacity, and fate of 197 elites in late Han China. A one standard deviation rise in clustering increases the probability of violent death by 19.1 percentage points. Clustering does not predict later military autonomy. Brokerage predicts co-optation, not death. Each purge wave left survivors less clustered and more militarized, arming the men who partitioned the empire.

Confidence under Audit: When Does Verification Help?
with Xuanyu Cai and Fengming Liu
Political Economy
This paper examines whether the effectiveness of a leader's public confidence signal is influenced by the chance of an external audit. We find that verification improves the signal when audits deter imitation by weak leaders without removing strong leaders' incentive to commit. Otherwise, its impact is limited. Too little auditing invites imitation, and too much auditing silences every leader, so attainable information drops sharply.

Genetic Screening in Cousin-Marriage Markets and the Recessive-Allele Stock
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
Genetic Economics
How does genetic information change who marries whom in cousin-marriage markets, and what happens to the recessive allele stock over time? We model a marriage market in which either partner can veto a risky cousin match and search outside the family. We show that sharper information lowers affected births today but raises the allele frequency later cohorts inherit; the reversal depends on affected survival. In equilibrium, affected births return to the flow set by mutation and selection, so information changes the allele frequency that sustains it. The findings suggest that this reversal comes from the marriage margin itself, so screening that protects children today can raise the burden of future generations.

Constitutional Choice before Residence
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
Political Economy
How should legal standards be set when the choice changes who lives where? We depict constitutional choice as a referendum held before residence, in which expected standards sort citizens and resident medians set the policy that follows. Voters anticipate the continuation, so identical fundamentals can retain the national code or devolve authority. We derive conditions under which majority rule departs from welfare, and under which a federal band uniquely implements integration but never positive diversity. Moving costs separate politics from welfare: low costs cause excessive divergence, intermediate costs block beneficial diversity, and high costs align both criteria at integration. We motivate the analysis with federal systems in which central rules limit local standards.

Biodiversity and Innovation
with Lyu Bing and Shun Wang
Why might market-based biological screening undermine future discovery? We study firms competing over a renewable stock whose discovery value fluctuates over time. Each firm values its own future access but ignores the option value its screening may destroy for rivals. In the verified classical symmetric equilibria we examine, this externality generates excessive screening when the stock is scarce. Recovery dynamics determine whether the distortion persists: if the stock can replenish quickly, the externality is muted; if not, firms screen faster than a producer-surplus planner and accelerate depletion. Our findings imply that in settings where screening destroys irreplaceable discovery potential, competition can systematically erode the shared resource base, and policy intervention may be warranted to preserve the option value for future research.

Vaccination and the Evolution of Genetic Susceptibility
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
We build a model of epidemic control with genetic selection. A planner chooses vaccine coverage; infection lowers fertility only among 𝑎𝑎 homozygotes, and vaccination shields the susceptibility allele from selection. Higher coverage raises the allele frequency inherited by the next cohort; the frequency is set by the epidemic and mutation. We show the trade-off between infection today and invasion tomorrow, and the balance depends on vaccine efficacy. Optimal policy may trade current protection against inherited susceptibility; then the social value of vaccination is negative. Optimal coverage is fine-tuned to balance protection today and invasion tomorrow.

分级诊疗制度
with Mingda Wang
在全面推进分级诊疗制度建设的背景下，如何设计最优的医疗分诊信息机制以提升资源配置效率，是学术界与决策者共同面临的问题。本文将贝叶斯说服模型引入健康经济学的研究框架，构建一个统一的理论模型，研究医疗资源在劳动密集型的初级医疗与资本密集型的专科医疗之间的配置，以及资源结构如何内生决定最优的分诊信息精度与信息披露政策。模型分析表明，医疗资源越有限，最优分诊阈值越严格；资源越充裕，分诊越宽松。在此基础上，本文依次引入四项制度与现实因素进行拓展。财政分权下，地方政府基于政绩考核的激励扭曲了医疗资本积累方向，迫使中央信息政策从柔性推荐退化为刚性配额。地区间医疗资源差异引发患者跨区流动，贫困地区的分诊政策存在抑制流出与转嫁成本的双向扭曲，中央统一信息政策配合跨区补偿机制可改善区域间配置效率。专科医生的需求创造行为在逐利动机足够强时瓦解政府信号，改革支付方式是恢复信息有效性的结构前提。数字技术的普及能够部分替代物质资源，使发展中经济体提前实现精准分诊。本文从信息设计理论与健康经济学的交叉视角出发，为中国分级诊疗改革的深层困境提供了系统性的理论解释与政策参考。
