01Reject & Resubmit · Quarterly Journal of Political Science
A Formal Model of Emotional Accountability
Formerly titled “A Formal Model of Climate Pledges and Accountability”, it is being reworked as a new story, retitled “A Formal Model of Emotional Accountability”, and will be revised together with new collaborators.
Political Economy
To be presented at the 98th Annual Meeting of the Southern Political Science Association, January 2027
We develop a dynamic model of climate pledges in which voter accountability and the commitments themselves are endogenous. Green and Brown governments alternate in power and announce pledges that their successors inherit. Voters feel anger when implemented effort falls short of an endogenously evolving reference point, and incumbents can strategically prime that anger. We show that moderate accountability leads Green incumbents to overpledge, forcing Brown successors to exert higher effort. Strong accountability, however, backfires: the emotional ratchet induces incumbents to abandon strategic pledging, producing a discontinuous drop in expected effort. We characterize this compliance cliff and relate pledge dynamics to observed reversals, offering a general account of decentralized cooperation.
02Revise & Resubmit · Cliometrica
How to Achieve Technological Leapfrogging: Evidence from China's Self-Strengthening Movement
with Fengming Liu (corresponding author) and Shun Wang
Reading China's Self-Strengthening Movement as a playbook for how late developers compress the catch-up gap.
Economic History
Presented at the 11th Quantitative History Annual Conference, Yantai, China
While extensive research diagnoses causes of technological backwardness, little examines how nations effectively bridge such gaps. How can late-industrializing economies accelerate technological catch-up? This study reinterprets China's Self-Strengthening Movement (SSM) to reveal how state-directed institutional adaptation compressed development timelines. Historical evidence shows that SSM-era military investments catalyzed catch-up through two mechanisms: strategic human capital accumulation and pervasive cognitive shifts dismantling technological conservatism. These institutional interventions created foundational capacity, enabling China's subsequent rapid electrification despite late entry. The findings illuminate the critical role of deliberate state plasticity in overcoming initial disadvantages. As China now faces its "Deepseek moment" — confronting new technological frontiers — this analysis offers a blueprint for developing economies seeking to engineer accelerated technological convergence. State-facilitated institutional adaptation emerges as a vital driver for latecomer advancement.
03Under Review
Political Purges and Elite Networks in Late Han China
with Fengming Liu, Fengsheng Xu and Yanxin Zhang
Economic History
How does uneven information shape a ruler's purges? We study late Han China. The court tracked social ties more closely than local military organization. In our model, current military command is observable, while latent mobilization is less legible. The model predicts that purges kill the well connected, spare the armed, and underweight latent capacity to raise troops. We test this with detailed network data, coded military command, and survival outcomes for 197 elites. A rise of one standard deviation in clustering is associated with an increase of about nineteen percentage points in the probability of political death. Brokerage predicts recruitment, not death. Elites without political-purge deaths were less connected and more likely to hold military command. Their ranks included the later warlords.

The Emperor's Lottery
with Fengming Liu, Yuxiang Wang and Fengsheng Xu
We study how states allocate talent when rulers must reward merit yet forgo case-by-case control. Qing China filled most county posts by lottery but reserved discretion for the hardest ones. Using newly digitized rosters for 1850–1864 and 1900–1912, we show strong and precise background-based sorting of officials across posts under the lottery channel, and no detectable sorting under discretionary provincial assignment. A commitment model explains how grouping candidates before the draw preserves matching gains under randomization. Freezing provincial destinations still keeps 45.2 and 41.2 percent of the nationwide assignment-score gain, yet realizing it within provinces demands reassigning at least 17.6 and 10.8 percent of appointments.

Hiding the Heir: Succession Secrecy and Dynastic Conflict in Europe
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
When a ruler's chosen heir becomes public knowledge shapes the violence of the transition that follows. Historians of monarchy have long noted a dilemma, since a designated heir becomes a rival center of power while an open succession invites plotting. We build a dynamic game in which a ruler installs an heir publicly or seals the choice and reveals it at death, and we derive an approximately optimal protocol of secrecy, a limited pledge window, and a named first coordinator. We show that secrecy deters challenges to a living target yet transfers the burden of coordination onto the accession, so a hidden heir inherits a document rather than a coalition. In a new hand-coded register of European successions from 1000 to 1800, public designation predicts more challenges before the ruler's death but shorter interregnums afterward. We test how this trade-off turns on the number of eligible princes, assess the binding role of protocol credibility, and discuss what the optimal design implies for transitions of power.

Self-Fulfilling Superstition: Zodiac Compatibility and Marriage in Qing China
with Xuanyu Cai and Fengsheng Xu
Superstition is usually modeled as a distortion. We show that it can instead select the equilibrium of a coordination game that spouses play after marriage, and that its value reverses with the thickness of the marriage market. Our setting is the Eight Banners population of Liaoning, recorded in registers that span 1749 to 1909. Because the zodiac compatibility class of a couple is fixed by two predetermined birth years, it is exogenous to marriage-market conditions. Compatibility predicts whom people marry and how long marriages last. A structural search and matching model separates the belief from assortative matching on lineage and wealth and implies that the belief raises surplus in thin markets and lowers it in thick markets.

Social Networks and Political Purges: Evidence from 1927 China
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
We ask how a regime that cannot observe political loyalty decides whom to purge. Using the 1927 Kuomintang purge of Chinese Communists, we build a person-level network of Whampoa Military Academy cadets from their native place, class cohort, and contact address, and match it to purge outcomes recovered from martyr registries and digitized Republican newspapers. A search-and-screening model predicts that victimization follows network exposure rather than true type. Leave-one-out peer instruments, exogenous variation in telegraph access, and the differing timing of the Nanjing and Wuhan purges identify a network contagion process behind guilt by association.

The Wind Price of Industrial Land
Air pollution is emitted where plants sit but inhaled where the wind carries it. We study optimal industrial siting when the regulator cannot observe firm types and can price land, not tonnes. The second best charges each site its discounted plume: a siting-rights auction with these reserve prices implements the optimum without screening firms. Calibrated to China's 2+26 cities, plume prices differ severalfold within a city and reorder the optimal industrial map; monitor-based standards instead push plants into regulatory shadows, where exposure per tonne is highest. Repricing siting buys more than a decade of tighter standards.

Zodiac Superstition, Fertility, and Human Capital: Evidence from Qing China
with Xuanyu Cai, Mingda Wang and Fengsheng Xu
Cultural beliefs about fate can stall or spur accumulation, yet their growth consequences are rarely identified. Using administrative registers from Qing Liaoning (1749–1909) and the twelve-year zodiac cycle, assigned mechanically by birth, we compare the same individual across spells that do or do not contain a zodiac year. Fertility rises during zodiac years, especially in sons, while mortality and flight do not; marriage accelerates among the young. The response reverses with lineage position: individuals with living parents and no sons increase births, others reduce them. Superstition thus operates as a merit market, channeling beliefs into fertility, human capital, and growth.

算法承诺、信息挤出与治理分权
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
当人工智能接管治理任务，哪些地方情境知识值得保留，又该经哪条渠道上报。
当前，数字政府建设加速推进，人工智能不断承担原本依赖地方判断的治理任务，如何在提升政务效率的同时维护地方长期积累的情境知识，正成为亟待解决的关键问题。本文聚焦数字政府治理中算法与地方知识的配置，通过构建中央与地方两级政府的信息配置模型，把地方知识刻画为可在多项任务间共用的投入，揭示任务归属经由共用投入影响人机比较优势的微观机理。研究发现，算法承担一项任务，既替换该任务的信息来源，也缩小其余任务调用地方知识的范围，因此过于零散的授权组合均劣于全算法配置，有价值的地方任务必须成组保留，地方组合的最高平均净信息价值决定全面部署算法的制度门槛；逐项部署即便逐轮更新都使人机表现与福利不降，仍可能停留在次优清单。本文进一步表明，地方判断既可以就地执行，也可以经报告进入集中执行，当报告成本较低而容量有限时，两种渠道能够共同维持单一渠道无法维持的知识生产；当报告可靠性有限时，信息价值较高的任务更值得保留地方执行，扩充报告容量会提高福利却降低知识投入。本文把数字政府中的人机分工从给定任务上的静态比较优势，拓展为知识用途与执行渠道的联合配置，为分类分级的算法治理提供理论依据。

Carrier Status, Cousin Offers, and Observability in Marriage-Market Search
with Xuanyu Cai and Fengming Liu
Micro Theory
How does a positive carrier-screening result change the choice between a confirmed first-cousin offer and unrelated marriage-market search? We depict this choice as a model in which a healthy adult who knows her genotype can surrender a kin offer for a costly search attempt. We derive conditions under which private carrier status raises the reservation cost of leaving the kin option, candidate observability reverses that ordering, and early and late disclosure differ. When candidates infer type from the absence of a signal, disclosure combines rejection with improved beliefs, so its effect on carrier search is ambiguous. We show that the ordering and the monotone disclosure response survive repeated search without recall.
01Under Review
Optimal Block Time with Staking Commitment
with Xuanyu Cai and Shun Wang
Micro Theory
How do protocol rules create commitment in a market where tokens pay no cash flows? We study a Stackelberg game where a staker locks tokens for integer blocks and commits to a deterministic liquidation schedule and a trader reacts to diffusion and jump news under block-dependent execution costs. The trader's unique affine best response makes the staker's schedule the metric projection of a fuel-only benchmark onto a cumulative-sales obstacle, yielding constraint losses and a test of whether the lock-up binds. At a fixed reward rate, every payoff-maximizing block time is strictly interior above a computable threshold. Higher rewards weakly lengthen commitment, while greater belief volatility weakly shortens the optimal block time. Protocol speed and credible commitment are one design problem.
02Under Review
Order Flow as a Resource: MEV, Mining, and the Market for Transaction Sequencing
with Beiyi Luo and Shun Wang
Micro Theory
Does the right to sequence transactions strengthen or undermine blockchain security? We model miners' ordering choices in a Proof-of-Work blockchain. Block value must be weighed against reorganization risk. The equilibrium displays strategic complementarity. Higher aggregate MEV attracts hash power, lowers reorg risk, and reinforces extraction incentives. This yields multiple equilibria: miners either extract no MEV or extract fully. Within a block, bot MEV crowds out retail transactions. In the long run, miner entry is neutral for the within-block allocation. Retail fees do not depend on aggregate hash power. Higher bot MEV therefore raises retail fees at all horizons. The sequencing rights market creates a trade-off between funding security and protecting users.

Credible Evaluation Design
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
Formerly titled “Optimal Evaluation Design for Robust Learning Agents under Distribution Shift”
An evaluator who must keep every test credible faces a trade-off between the strongest authorized screen and the temptation of AI agents to game the benchmark rather than serve deployment.
We study information design in which an authority sets evaluation frequencies to steer AI agents away from benchmark gaming, the pursuit of high leaderboard scores in testing at the expense of performance in deployment. Credibility bounds each authorized protocol, so the design trades two burdens: the strongest protocol permitted and the average departure from deployment. We characterize this frontier and show that the burden minimizers generally differ. For screens that preserve labeled-task composition, we derive sharp bounds linking the two burdens. Under average log-frequency accounting, amplifying rare failures rewards concentrating pressure in occasional strong tests, whereas suppressing rare easy contexts makes mild screening optimal. We give convex certificates for both burdens and conditions under which new protocols lower the burden of an existing standard.
Micro Theory
Data Progress and the Confidence Trap
Firms route forecasting, diagnosis, and credit screening to AI models whose reported confidence sets how much human effort to retain. We cast it as a delegation problem where data coverage drives accuracy and overconfidence. Further data can raise accuracy and weaken overconfidence on every task yet lower the payoff from following their reports, because improved answers pull tasks across two protection boundaries too soon. We give an exact loss for premature crossings, show how task density reverses the return to coverage progress, and derive the calibration speed progress requires. A beneficial upgrade can pass through harmful intermediate versions. Under pooled evidence, selective approval prices a payoff guarantee, small concessions cutting worst-case regret at a square-root rate until both losses balance.

The Design of Scientific Gatekeeping
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
Generative AI lets authors produce publication-ready manuscripts almost for free, and no editor can referee the resulting flood. We build a theory of scientific gatekeeping in which a model is dual-use: it raises the genuine output of able researchers and cheapens the packaging that makes weak work look strong, and the gatekeeper cannot separate the two. Authors differ in research ability and AI fluency, while model capability grows across generations. This creates a discernibility externality that no author internalizes. Three counterintuitive results follow. Raising the acceptance bar can lower the genuine quality of the published record, a more capable model can reduce welfare even as it raises output, and mandatory disclosure of AI use can backfire. Optimal policy therefore concentrates on the output side, combining submission fees, verification, and ex post liability rather than detection.

Designing Correlated Evidence in Teams
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
An AI team reads shared sources, and each source sharpens every judgment while carrying one common error into all of them, so limiting circulation diversifies team error rather than losing efficiency.
Large language model agents share reusable evidence: the same retrieved context feeds several calls that each return a judgment for a decision. Useful content sharpens every judgment, yet a common omission travels to every user, so sharing links errors at their source. We ask how a principal should procure these sources, assign their audiences, and weight the decisions. For homogeneous sources under equal weights we construct the optimal architecture and reveal a homogenization trap: buying every source is optimal, yet giving every source to every agent is strictly suboptimal even under optimal aggregation. This reversal implies that limiting circulation diversifies team error rather than wastes evidence. We further show that stronger fallback decisions restore supplier financing without improving full-team accuracy.

Limit theorems for non-clairvoyant dynamic pricing in random environments
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
We study repeated selling of a perishable good whose value distribution is governed by an unknown stationary random environment. A non-clairvoyant seller sets prices from past observations, while a clairvoyant oracle knows the environment law. We prove that non-clairvoyant regret vanishes and revenue reaches the oracle level almost surely. We establish a central limit theorem for regret and identify its limiting variance through a Green-Kubo formula built from the optimal revenue influence function and the environment Dirichlet form. A large deviation principle shows that the classical worst case guarantee of one half is exponentially rare. A mean field limit produces a Gibbs stationary measure for pooled pricing. Non-clairvoyance is asymptotically costless except under exponentially unlikely adverse environments.

Optimal Screening of Private Losses on Contagion Networks
with Fengming Liu and Hao Wen
Relief that halts a loss spreading along a network also erases the only evidence a principal has about private losses, so the optimal design keeps some contagion alive to verify claims.
We study the optimal design of relief for privately observed losses that spill over along a network. Relief protects claimants, yet the contagion it suppresses is also the principal's only evidence about private losses, so protection and verification compete. We introduce the relief multiplier, which aggregates contagion-weighted social values, and the corresponding verification capacity of each node. The optimal mechanism exploits contagion as a monitoring technology and attains the first-best whenever losses are sufficiently affiliated. Hubs may receive less relief than peripheral nodes, and stronger contagion can raise welfare. The optimum solves a fixed point whose distributed, monotone iteration converges to the unique constrained-efficient allocation. We derive closed forms and testable comparative statics.

Optimal Sequential Interdiction of a Loitering Munition Swarm
A defender must protect assets against a swarm of loitering munitions whose payloads are hidden, so each object is either a warhead or a decoy, while interceptors are scarce and one spent on a decoy is wasted. We model the conflict as a sequential screening game in which the defender observes noisy signals of each object's type and decides when to engage, and the attacker jointly chooses the decoy fraction and the strike time. We show that the defender's optimal interdiction policy is an index rule on the posterior threat probability, and we characterize the equilibrium through a tractable convex program. We further identify a saturation threshold beyond which defense collapses, show that the attacker's optimal decoy fraction is interior whenever deception is cheap, and derive comparative statics in the costs of decoys and interceptors. Numerical experiments calibrated to loitering munition data confirm the index policy and the saturation effect.

Screening and Matching in Supply Chains
with Xuanyu Cai and Shun Wang
We study supply chains in which buyers match with suppliers whose match productivity is persistent and privately observed. We show that the second best allocation is a Monge–Kantorovich plan whose transport cost equals the information rent, and we characterize the dual potentials as prices of incumbency. This yields a two regime characterization. With low persistence, matching is assortative and rents are small. With high persistence, rents compound, the sorting premium vanishes, and matching reverses. A key welfare finding is that a uniform tariff can raise output while diversification mandates reduce it. The analysis shows how persistence alone reshapes sorting.

Too Connected: The Value of Habitat Connectivity
with Xuanyu Cai and Mingda Wang
Species must find habitat that suits them, and connectivity sets how fast they find it. Connectivity is nonetheless credited with raising occupancy everywhere, ignoring the crowding that follows when many species reach the same patches. We model habitat as a market with search frictions and solve for equilibrium biodiversity. Biodiversity is not monotone in connectivity: past a threshold, more corridors let mobile generalists displace residents. A randomized corridor experiment and a twenty-year breeding bird panel confirm that threshold. Calibrated to United States land cover, connectivity raises diversity by 10 percent, worth about US$30 billion a year.

02Working Paper
Global cooling and local warming pace warfare across ten centuries
Science
Warfare has punctuated every century of the past millennium, yet its climatic drivers remain disputed. Existing studies regress conflict on local weather one scale at a time and cannot separate a global temperature shock from regional deviations. Here we assemble ten centuries of palaeoclimate reconstructions and georeferenced battles across Eurasia and the Americas, and resolve their coupling across time scales. Global temperature co-varies with warfare at centennial scales, whereas local temperature deviations drive it at decadal scales. Phase analysis places volcanic and solar forcing ahead of both. Temperature, not politics alone, paces the rhythm of history.
03Working Paper
Unequal gains from paying for forests
with Mingda Wang
Science
Payments for forest conservation are a pillar of global climate policy, yet whether they deliver their benefits fairly, and to whom, remains unknown. Reanalyzing a landmark randomized trial in Uganda, we show that a uniform payment to forest owners conceals large inequality in its effects. Using causal forests, we find that although every owner benefits, the gains are extremely skewed and that the top six percent of owners achieve conservation gains more than triple the average. This variation reflects baseline characteristics rather than the program itself. The strongest predictor is not poverty, education, or timber income but baseline forest cover, which shapes effects in an inverted U pattern: owners with moderate endowments facing active deforestation pressure gain the most. Data driven targeting rules can raise the benefit cost ratio by a factor of two to three, but the most profitable rules channel payments to the wealthiest owners, deepening the very inequality that motivated the program. Simple, transparent decision rules deliver comparable efficiency while spreading payments across the socioeconomic distribution. The efficiency equity trade off in climate finance is therefore not inevitable: how we target matters as much as whether we target.
